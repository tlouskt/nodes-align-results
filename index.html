Protein alignment across Qwen sizes

Updated: 28 October 2026, 11:45 AM Pacific. New families are published as they finish.

Alignment measures how often two models choose the same nearest protein neighbors. It ranges from 0 to 1. Qwen size increases from left to right, from 0.6B to 14B; B means billion parameters. Each family has two plots: alignment with Qwen, with one line each for ProtT5 and ESM3, and the difference between those alignments. Each point is the mean shared-neighbor count divided by 10. All plots use the 512-token protocol.

ProtT5 ↔ QwenESM3 ↔ QwenRaw difference = ProtT5 − ESM3

Gap (percentage points) = 100 × [alignment(Qwen, ProtT5) − alignment(Qwen, ESM3)].
The difference plot shows the raw gap: +0.01 = +1 percentage point. Positive means ProtT5 aligns more with Qwen; negative means ESM3 aligns more.

Each family needs all five Qwen sizes and both protein models.
